Every name the live pages watch (150 most-traded coins, 300 most-traded stocks and ETFs, the focus futures), on the 5m, 15m, 1h, 4h, daily and weekly, re-scanned in a loop (a pass takes 10 to 15 minutes). An EQ here is a sideways stretch: over the last 30 bars the highest high and lowest low are at most 4 normal bars apart and both edges were touched at least twice. That height test is made when the box forms, so an older box can read taller or shorter than 4 against today's bar size: both numbers are in the table. It stays alive until a bar closes beyond an edge. Where shows where the newest price sits inside the box (left edge = floor, right = ceiling). Green rows are at the floor, red rows at the ceiling: the places to get positioned for the break. The call is which way the break is expected, from three reads that agree two out of three: the chart above, what the lows and highs are doing inside the box, and which edge has been tested more. In the 4-year study an UP call broke up … of the time and a DOWN call broke down …. The tables at the bottom say what each read and each trade is worth. Drawn examples: /eqcharts.
